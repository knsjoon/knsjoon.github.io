AHEAD: Anticipatory Hand-Driven Teleoperation

Predicting human intent from natural hand motion so robots can begin acting before an explicit command.

Authors
Seok Joon Kim, Joonho Lee, Federica Spinola, Taein Kwon, Mohsen Moghaddam
Topics
VR, intent prediction, HRI, digital twins
Status
IROS 2026 submission

The research

Faster reactions, lower operator effort.

Direct teleoperation gives precise control but demands constant correction. Goal-based control reduces that burden, yet forces the robot to wait. AHEAD combines the strengths of both.

Inside a VR digital twin, an operator performs natural pick-and-place motions. AHEAD processes recent 3D hand and head signals together with scene context, predicts the intended object and target slot, and converts those predictions into stable robot goals.

01

Anticipatory control

A preview-and-commit policy initiates conservative motion early and commits once intent becomes reliable.

02

Hand-driven intent prediction

Attention-based classifiers infer grasp and placement goals from streaming hand joints, head pose, and scene geometry.

03

Human-subject validation

A 15-participant study found reduced robot reaction time and operator workload relative to the baselines.
